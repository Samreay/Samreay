## Blurb

Gods. Devils. Men. Red will surpass them all.

The guards say there are no children in the Moonstone Mines, a place walked only by men condemned to slavery and death. An unyielding curse ensures none can depart its dark depths.

And yet, a child named Red awakens in the mines with no memory. Haunted by enigmatic dreams, he melds with the slaves, enthralled by tales of the world above. He learns how to cultivate and hunt the deadly monsters that infest the mines, but escape remains an impossibility.

Until escape is the only option left.

However, the path to freedom is steeped in peril. With the weight of the curse and a growing demonic corruption within, Red's pursuit of strength isn't just a desire—it's a desperate necessity. He must rise to unimaginable heights, outpacing the curse's deadly grasp and taming the demonic power before it consumes him.

Accompany Red as he grows from a mere slave into a man who will forever change the world around him. Gods, devils, and even darker adversaries rise to challenge his ascent to the pinnacle of power.

Only by mastering the dark forces that threaten to overtake him can he truly be free.

## Thoughts

*As of writing this review, I'm halfway through book three.*

So I'm a sucker for nice cover art, and I decided to pick this series up after seeing a link to book three's cover art. It's also been a hot minute since I've read a cultivation-esque novel, so that's nice too. I've put it down for the moment because _another_ beautiful cover caught my eye (The Lich King Reincarnates as a Baby), but I'll probably come back once I've finished that one off.

Anyway, let's focus on this story. Classic underground mining operation opening, calling me back to **Way of the Shaman**, which I read so many years ago. Like normal, slave mines go to shit, and for most of the story we follow our protag, Red, on his desperate and lonesome attempt to leave the mines without being eaten by any of the countless horrors that infest them. When he does get out, he (reluctantly) joins a tiny little sect, but for those wanting some traditional sect life and cultivation tropes, this is very westernised, so expect it to play out more like a very chaotic found-family (at least for now) than your mountains-spanning ruthless-disciple-filled traditional sect.

There are some overarching mysteries and plot points, like the moon, the crimson mist, the necromancer, and a few other things I won't spoil, which is very nice to have. The mysteries are not presented and then explained in the next chapter. They're long-term goals and intrigue that help pull you in. The more worldly plot points (bandits, etc) are serviceable as a mechanism to help advance plot and power.

Well, more plot than power I guess. Early in book one, before Red departs the mining operation, he's opened two of the twelve spiritual veins that form the lowest rung on the cultivation ladder. Above that are the Lesser Ring Realm, then Greater Ring, then the third, fourth, fifth, etc realms above that. At the time of putting this down (60% of the way in book three), Red has opened two more veins I think, for four out of twelve. It might even be five, if I've forgotten. But he's not even close to being done with the very first layer of cultivation, so this is definitely, _definitely_ not an OP MC story, nor one with the MC growing quickly. Many stories (including my own, probably motivated by **Cradle** doing this so well) effectively follow a "one-realm-per-book" style, as the Big Conflict upon which each book hinges necessitates some Big Powerup. Not this story. That's alright. A slow burn is still a good burn, but some may find this too slow.

Because Red is still so weak, I can't really talk about the power system and how well it works yet. Red is stronger and faster than a normal 10-year-old, but... uh... that doesn't mean much. Every adult is. The crimson mist lets him detect lifeforce though, which is clutch. Ask me again in like ten books.

Prose is decent. Not purple, simple, sometimes a bit verbose. It's not trying to be fancy or poignant though, which I like.

Characters and dialogue are where I think the story struggles a little bit. Red is a 10-year-old that doesn't sound like a ten-year-old at all. Example dialogue:

> “I think the more important question is why she needs to be worried about you in the first place,” Red answered.

And

> “Of course, this might change in the future as you learn and become stronger, but realizing your own limitations is an important part of survival. If you feel you can’t accomplish something by yourself, either don’t attempt it unless necessary, or rely on someone else you know can do it. This is something you’ll have to learn too.”

Other characters lampshade this a bit:

> “I just find it funny.” Rog shrugged, looking between Red and Rimold. “I have a hard time telling who’s the kid and who’s the adult in your conversations.”

But still, I can't tell where this sits on the scale between "classic child-speaks-like-an-adult issue found all over the genre" and "actually there's a good reason for this and it'll be revealed in five books' time". So you've got Red, speaking like an adult, very detached, clinical, and emotionless in his behaviour and words (which also makes him harder to engage with as a reader, alas), but then many of the other characters seem to all sound the same with one idiosyncrasy on top. Hector YELLS. Allen stutters. Eiwin calls people by their surname. But apart from that, they mostly have the same method of speaking. I.e., most people speak to Red tersely and call him "kid". They speak to Hector resentfully, and call him "old man". I noticed this most when I got some characters confused in tagless dialogue sections, because their voices weren't distinct. Also their names are similar - in the scene I'm thinking of, we just have character references to Allen, Red, Rog, Rimold, Reinhart, and they're asking about Ricard. That's alotta Rs. It's a nice letter though, my favourite actually. I demand Allen be renamed to Rallen!

Anyway, it's a fun read for those who enjoy slower-burn westernised cultivation stories with a decent mix of Red doing his own thing and him being part of a Sect party.
