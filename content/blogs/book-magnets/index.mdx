My fridge now has a tiny bookshelf, and my boys' artwork is held up by some of my favourite books. The idea came from this [Instagram reel by Mossy Burrow Creations](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfhyWL4N-8v/).

Of course, I didn't just use someone else's design, because there was a big issue! No, it wasn't paying for a design, that's fair enough. See, I've got a newborn and a two-year-old in the house, and the idea of a magnet (or a dozen) held in place only by some glue and some paper... it's not good. I wanted the magnet sealed inside the plastic so not even God would be able to pry them out. And thus, this entire journey.

## Making the covers

A lot of the progression fantasy and LitRPG I read comes out as ebooks. I love ebooks, but this means authors generally only have the front cover available for me to take off Royal Road or Amazon. A tiny book still needs a spine and a back. The easiest way would have been to just ask AI to generate appropriate spines (and black backs), except the idea of feeding an author's cover art into GenAI is icky. Less icky is asking AI to write code, so I was happy to use AI coding tools to help write some JavaScript that picks colours from the cover and adds a bit of texture and lettering in the browser. It's nothing too fancy (and thus why there are options to pick colours manually).

That turned into the [Book Magnet Workshop](/reviews/book-magnets/), where you can pick your books and download a sheet of full wraps to print. For me, this was two 8x10inch prints from Kmart, for a grand total of $8.

## Printing the books and shelf

I modelled the book in Fusion 360, providing a cutout for some ceramic magnets I already had from years and years ago. Then I decided the books deserved a bookshelf of their own, so I modelled that too. I think the link may be view-only on my Fusion plan, so I've included both print files here.

The book 3MF has filament changes for the white pages and a pause to drop in the magnet before the printer closes it up. I stacked two 18 × 3 mm ceramic discs ([like these ones](https://www.amazon.com.au/Grtard-Adhesive-0-7x0-12in-Workshop-Projects/dp/B0DFLS25YZ)) for a stronger hold, so the stack is 6 mm thick. The shelf file has its own pause for a rectangular 60 × 10 × 3 mm neodymium magnet, which I also had lying around. [These are the ones](https://www.amazon.com.au/Wukong-60x10x3mm-Magnetic-Adhesive-Whiteboard/dp/B0BTHMPNSD).
